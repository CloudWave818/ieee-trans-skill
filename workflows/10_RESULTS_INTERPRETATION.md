# 10 Results Interpretation

## Entry

Require actual results, the experiment setup, metric definitions, baseline conditions, and claim links. Planned or missing values cannot enter result prose.

## Paragraph architecture

Use the elements supported by the data:

1. **Observation** — the salient pattern, not every number.
2. **Comparison** — the relevant comparator under fair conditions.
3. **Quantitative evidence** — values, uncertainty, or effect size actually present.
4. **Explanation** — a bounded account tied to method, assumption, experiment, or data.
5. **Mechanism** — how a tested component may produce the pattern when the evidence supports that inference.
6. **Scientific implication** — what the result changes about the question or claim.

## Controls

- Distinguish observation from causal explanation.
- State exceptions, failure cases, and trade-offs; do not cherry-pick only the best setting.
- Avoid repeated “Fig. X shows” or “Table X shows” openings followed by number transcription.
- Compare normalized conditions and disclose unavailable statistics.
- Use “supports,” “is consistent with,” or scoped language when direct causal evidence is absent.
- Do not promote a simulation result to real-world deployment or an in-distribution test to strong generalization.

## Exit

Each results paragraph answers a scientific question, cites its asset, respects claim boundaries, and exposes rather than hides contradictory evidence.
